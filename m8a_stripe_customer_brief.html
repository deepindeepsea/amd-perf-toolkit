m8a.2xlarge-equivalent shape — single CCD, 8-core slice

To model what a customer running on a small m8a.2xlarge (8 vCPUs ≈ one CCD) would see, we pinned 7 Puma workers to cores 0–6 (CCD0) and the wrk client to core 7 (same CCD). This emulates the noisy-neighbor-free best case for an 8-vCPU instance shape: tight L3 locality, no cross-CCD traffic, no IOD contention.

Per-CCD sanity check: 23.5k RPS peak on a single CCD matches the N=1 baseline (24.6k, with wrk on a different CCD). Co-locating wrk on core 7 of CCD0 costs only ~4% throughput but improves p99 at low concurrency (3.86 vs 7.07 ms) because the kernel doesn't have to cross the SDF fabric for loopback packets. For sizing m8a.2xlarge in production: assume ~23k RPS peak Rails Puma "Hello World" and de-rate to ~14k RPS for full-stack ActiveRecord+JSON. Target steady-state 60–65% utilization to preserve sub-10 ms p99 → plan for ~9k sustained RPS per 2xlarge.

Performance architecture — why M8A wins 2×

Frequency split

M8A holds 4.46 GHz at N=1, 3.99 GHz at N=8. Genoa-X under sustained all-core load is ~3.2–3.6 GHz. Frequency alone explains ~1.17–1.24× of the gap. The remaining ~1.6–1.9× is microarchitecture.

µarch split

Zen5 doubles decode width (4→8), doubles op-cache, adds a second branch-predictor pipe. PMC shows Frontend Bound at ~100% slots — Rails dispatch is heavily branchy with a 2.75% misp rate. Zen5's wider fetch and improved TAGE-class predictor are the structural advantage.

Why does 3D V-Cache buy nothing here?

L2 hit rate is ~80% across all N. Per-Puma-worker hot footprint already fits in the 1 MB private L2 + 32 MB CCD-local L3. The extra 64 MB stacked V-Cache helps when *cores in the same CCD share working sets* (databases, in-memory analytics). Puma workers are fork-isolated — no shared mutable state, no V-Cache benefit.
Negative result: SO_REUSEPORT failed. Linux kernel hashes 4-tuple; on localhost (src IP = dst IP, fixed dst port), all wrk connections collapse to a single Puma instance. Flat ~25k RPS regardless of N. Localhost benchmark artifact — would spread correctly behind ALB/HAProxy or with cross-host traffic. Do not use REUSEPORT for future localhost benches.

Methodological caveat

"Hello World" Rails loads dispatch, middleware, Puma loop, Ruby VM, allocator, and Linux net stack. It does not load ActiveRecord, JSON serialization, TLS, Sidekiq, or business logic — which together typically eat 30–60% of CPU time on Stripe-style payments. State the 2.0–2.3× per-CCD M8A win as an upper bound; expect 1.4–1.8× on representative full-stack workloads. Stripe should re-validate on shadow-traffic replay before sizing the final fleet.

Recommendation & rollout

Adopt m8a.metal-24xl as the next-gen general-purpose tier for Rails synchronous-API services.

Defer Graviton4 to a parallel workstream — may win on $/RPS in 12–18 months once Stripe's gem matrix is audited.
  • Phase 1 (4 wk): Shadow-traffic validation on 4× m8a in us-east-1. Confirm real-app RPS gain ≥ 1.4× vs m7a baseline, p99 within SLO.
  • Phase 2 (2 wk): 5% canary in us-east-1. Gate on error rate, p99, GC pauses, customer SLOs.
  • Phase 3 (6 wk): Region-by-region replacement, oldest m7i first.
  • Parallel: Graviton4 evaluation — gem audit, CI arm64 spike.

Open follow-ups before final customer presentation

  • Run matched Genoa-X PMC at N=8 to numerically confirm the freq/IPC decomposition.
  • Multi-CCD or cross-host wrk client to lift the 150k loopback ceiling and probe N=10–12 server scaling.
  • Real Stripe-shape workload: synthetic ActiveRecord query + 4 KB JSON response. Closes the methodology gap.
  • Repeat with EDP / Savings Plan pricing rather than list rate.
